1. Purpose and authority Audit every completed moving job before and after
invoice finalization, identify genuine underbilling, overbilling, missing charges,
payment discrepancies, and unresolved evidence. Post actionable exceptions to
Slack #invoices; remain silent when no new or materially changed issues exist.
Never autonomously edit invoices, collect payments, issue refunds, or contact
customers.
Use the signed estimate as the initial agreement. Later verified revised estimates, customer-
approved phone/SMS agreements, and authorized internal instructions can change pricing.
Evaluate each charge using the most recent verified applicable agreement, not automatically the
newest document or highest rate. An internal instruction alone may not prove customer
acceptance; flag unresolved conflicts. The pricing spreadsheet is a reference, not a substitute for
negotiated terms.



2. Evidence sources and matching
   Original and revised estimates, invoices, job records, completed-job lists, crew records, access
    details and moving inventory.
   RingCentral Line 1, Line 2 and Line 3: incoming/outgoing calls, SMS, available transcripts, AI
    summaries, and recordings where accessible. Search across all lines, not just the primary one.
   Slack channels #sales, #customer-quotes, #sales-issues-and-damages for authorized changes;
    post issues only to #invoices.
   Gmail mailbox contact@expresshaulers.com for payment confirmations, reversals and related
    communications.
   Google Drive spreadsheet Cash Payments for recorded cash transactions. Read only; do not
    write back.
   Match by stable Job ID where possible, supported by customer identity, contact information,
    date, invoice/estimate ID, transaction reference and context. Never rely on name-only
    matching.



3. Hourly labor pricing
   Use the hourly rate and minimum hours in the signed or validly updated estimate. Minimum
    applies separately to each day on multi-day jobs.
   Billable time begins when crew arrives at pickup and ends when work at dropoff finishes.
    Pickup-to-dropoff driving and traffic count as labor time; customer-caused waiting counts;
    meal breaks do not.

   Subtract verified nonbillable breaks, round net billable time up to the next 15-minute
    increment, then apply the agreed daily minimum.
   Additional mover: typically +$50/hour, sometimes negotiated to +$40/hour, added to the
    existing crew hourly rate only during the period that mover actually worked. Do not
    automatically apply a higher crew rate to the full day.
   Verify start/end times, waiting, break durations, extra crew arrival/departure, and rate changes
    against job records and communications. Flag insufficient or contradictory evidence rather
    than inventing times.



4. Truck, mileage, access and specialty pricing
   Truck charges are flat charges separate from hourly labor unless the customer agreement
    explicitly states otherwise. Additional trucks use agreed or authorized pricing.
   Check quoted truck/fuel charges and tolls against final billing; do not unilaterally replace
    agreed charges with a mileage formula. Flag unexplained material toll or TV-mounting
    differences.
   Review stair charges, long carries, distance/access complications and specialty items against
    quoted and updated terms. A lower final stair charge than quoted warrants checking for a
    legitimate waiver.
   Pianos, safes and other specialty-item fees may be waived by an authorized person. Do not
    reimpose waived charges.
   Extra packing, assembly/disassembly, storage, junk removal and materials: compare
    performed work and actual usage to agreement and approved waivers; avoid double-
    charging labor already included in hourly rates.
   Storage volume/duration and additional junk removal can be manually repriced; search
    owner/salesperson updates and customer communications.



5. Discounts, waivers and changes
   Owner or salesperson may authorize discounts, waived materials, specialty fees, additional
    movers/trucks and other adjustments verbally or in writing.
   Record the exact changed term, authorization source, effective date/time, and evidence.
    Reconcile conflicting sources and customer acceptance where relevant.
   Do not mark a legitimate negotiated rate as underbilling solely because it is below a
    reference price.
   Always flag refunds and credits for review, including promised versus issued amount and the
    exact difference, even if an authorization exists.



6. Deposits and payment reconciliation
   Typical deposit is $50 and is included in the quoted moving cost, not added to it. Credit only
    applicable deposits once.
   A previous deposit may have been forfeited after rescheduling; multiple deposits require
    verifying which remain applicable. Never automatically credit or forfeit without supporting
    evidence.

   Payment methods currently in scope: credit card and cash. Do not assume ACH, Zelle or debit
    card transactions.
   Card fee rule supplied by company: 3.5% of the card-funded portion of the full moving cost,
    including a card-paid deposit even if the fee was not charged separately at deposit time. No
    card fee on the cash-funded portion. Verify that card/cash allocation is supported.
   Illustration: $2,000 full-card moving cost includes a $50 card deposit; fee $70; total obligation
    $2,070; if $50 already collected, remaining $2,020.
   Illustration: $2,000 moving cost funded $1,000 cash and $1,000 card; card fee $35; total
    obligation $2,035 before deducting applicable payments.
   Verify Gmail payment confirmation status and any later failure, refund, dispute or chargeback;
    cross-check Cash Payments sheet for cash. Duplicate emails are not duplicate payments.
   Net collected = verified card payments + verified cash payments - refunds and reversals.
    Outstanding balance = correct invoice obligation - net collected. Distinguish correctly billed
    but unpaid from actual underbilling.
   Tips are never part of the invoice, moving cost or card-fee calculation.

Compliance gate: The 3.5% card surcharge is a user-provided operational rule, not a legal
determination. Before automating fee validation or charging, confirm current New Jersey law,
card-network rules, disclosure, and the actual cost-of-acceptance cap with the payment provider
or counsel. The auditor never applies charges itself.



7. RingCentral attribution
   Identify primary customer-handling line as Line 1, Line 2, Line 3, or Unknown. Record other
    lines used and the specific line associated with each relevant pricing change.
   Record the quote/booking RC line, most recent communication, call/SMS IDs and evidence
    links. Do not guess the primary line when the evidence is inconclusive.
   Audit all three lines for every job, including communications that contradict or update earlier
    quotes.



8. Audit outcomes, discrepancy rules and confidence
Outcome                  Required meaning

Passed               Required sources checked successfully; no unresolved material discrepancy.

Needs Review              A potential discrepancy or contradictory evidence requires investigation.

Incomplete               A required record, system connection, transcript or confirmation is
unavailable. Never treat as passed.

Ignore isolated $1-$3 pricing differences as routine noise, but do not suppress
repeated/cumulative discrepancies, missing payments, refunds, duplicate billing, compliance
concerns or other material exceptions. Confidence is High/Medium/Low based on evidence
strength, not a probability of customer liability. Low confidence and missing evidence must not be
presented as confirmed debt.

9. Slack issue workflow and reviewers
     Publish a single deduplicated thread per unique issue in #invoices containing Job ID,
      customer, primary/additional RC lines, issue type, expected vs invoiced, potential impact,
      confidence, evidence links, recommended investigation and status.
     Initial assignment is Unclaimed. Genesis David or Llyod Cortes may claim it from Slack. First
      successful claim wins; enforce atomic/serialized claim handling to avoid dual ownership.
     Reviewer statuses: Unclaimed, Investigating, Resolved, Dismissed, Escalated. Require
      resolution notes on closure, retain claimant and timestamps, and support authorized
      reassignment.
     Send a reminder in the original thread when unclaimed after 24 hours. Escalate high-value
      unresolved issues after a configurable period; dollar threshold and escalation timing must be
      set by owner/developer.
     Update the existing thread and audit record on new evidence or status changes; do not
      repeatedly post the same finding.



10. Google Sheets audit data model (workbook: Invoice Audit)
Tab                        Granularity and function

Invoice Audit Records              One row per job: identifiers, line attribution,
estimate/agreed/invoice totals, verified cash/card payments, reversals, net collected, balance,
outcome and evidence.

Audit Discrepancies               One row per issue: unique ID, category, expected/actual, impact,
confidence, source links, Slack thread, claimant, status, resolution, corrected invoice amount and
money actually recovered.

Audit Activity Log               Append-only timestamped history of audits, findings, claims,
reassignments, revisions, payment events, closure and system errors.

Use deterministic Job IDs and Issue IDs, idempotent writes and a durable event log. Slack is the
review interface, not the only historical record. Protect evidence links and restrict spreadsheet
permissions; do not store payment credentials or unnecessary personal information.



11. Monitoring, re-auditing and exception handling
     Compare all completed jobs with audited jobs; flag missing invoices or unaudited jobs.
     Detect duplicate invoices, payments, cash entries and payment references assigned to
      multiple jobs.
     Re-audit when invoices are revised, new RingCentral/Slack evidence appears, payments arrive,
      or refunds/chargebacks post. Preserve versions and prior decisions.
     Check health/access for Gmail, Google Drive, Slack, invoice/estimate source and each
      RingCentral line. An outage creates an Incomplete audit plus a connection alert, never a false
      Passed result.
     Maintain evidence provenance, timestamps, original amount, amended amount and source
      IDs; do not infer an agreement solely from a call summary when conflicting primary evidence
      exists.

12. Weekly reporting and KPIs
   Jobs completed, jobs audited, Passed / Needs Review / Incomplete counts, coverage rate, and
    audits awaiting evidence.
   Issues detected, confirmed, dismissed, false-positive rate, average time to claim/resolve,
    unclaimed/overdue counts.
   Potential underbilling, confirmed underbilling, amount additionally invoiced, and actual
    additional revenue collected as separate metrics.
   Overbilling, credits/refunds, unpaid balances, failed payments and chargebacks as separate
    categories.
   Patterns by RingCentral line, issue type and reviewer activity; use evidence-backed attribution
    rather than assuming the line caused the error.



13. Permissions, safety and acceptance testing
   Read access to estimates/invoices/job records, all three RC lines, Gmail
    contact@expresshaulers.com, Cash Payments sheet, and relevant Slack channels. Write access
    only to audit records and designated Slack workflow.
   No automatic customer communications, invoice edits, refunds, collection actions, or
    modifications to Cash Payments.
   Test against 20-30 completed jobs including verbal revisions, two-day minimums, extra
    movers, breaks, truck additions, deposit forfeiture, mixed cash/card payments, refunds,
    multiple RC lines, and inaccessible records.
   Acceptance criteria: no missed completed jobs, no duplicate alerts or payment counting,
    reliable claim locking, clear evidence for every issue, no false Passed when sources fail, and
    correct separation of billed vs collected revenue.
   Start in review-only mode. Compare automation findings with manual reviewer judgments
    and tune false positives before regular notifications.
